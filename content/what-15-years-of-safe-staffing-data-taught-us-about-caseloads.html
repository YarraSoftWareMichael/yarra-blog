Safe staffing in community nursing has always come down to one question: does the person holding the caseload actually know what's on it?

For years the honest answer in a lot of trusts was no, not really. Caseloads were held in people's heads, in spreadsheets, in the judgement of a team lead who'd been doing the job for fifteen years and could feel when a patch was overloaded. That knowledge is real, but it doesn't scale, it doesn't transfer when someone goes on leave, and it doesn't hold up when a commissioner asks for evidence.

ECAT was built with Belfast Trust in 2010 to close that gap, not by replacing clinical judgement but by giving it something to point at. Right Nurse, Right Task, Right Time only works as a principle if you can see activity against complexity and dependency in the same view. Once a caseload holder can see that, the conversation with a commissioner stops being "trust us" and starts being "here's the evidence."

Fifteen years on, the pattern holds outside district nursing too. Health visiting, children's nursing, any service built around caseloads and variable patient complexity runs into the same wall: judgement that lives in one person's head is a single point of failure. The fix isn't a bigger spreadsheet. It's a system that makes the judgement visible, comparable, and auditable without taking the clinician out of the decision.

That's the thread running through most of what we build at Yarra: software that evidences what good clinicians already know, rather than software that tells them what to do.
